
- Would pay for solution embedded in their platform
- Features competitors don't have (Touchplan vs Oracle, Procore vs Trimble)

## PART 5: PREMORTEM - WHAT COULD GO WRONG

### Critical Assumption #1: They Want This at All
**Failure Probability**: 40%

**What could go wrong**:
- Procore/Touchplan already have roadmap priorities (might not fit)
- They're building competing feature in-house (they have resources)
- They think it's "nice to have" not "must have"
- Legal/compliance hesitation (accuracy liability on them)
- Integration complexity worse than expected ($500K vs $100K)
- "Not invented here" syndrome (prefer to build themselves)

**Evidence this might fail**:
- Large software companies often resist integrations
- They have APIs but don't always pursue partnerships
- Accuracy liability is real concern (if RegGuard is wrong, they get blamed)
- Competitive pressure might make them prefer in-house solution
- Product roadmap might already be full for next 18 months

**Impact if it fails**:
- No partnership deal
- Revenue strategy pivots to freemium or direct sales
- Year 5 revenue drops from $2-5M to $700K-1M

---

### Critical Assumption #2: Contractors Will Adopt It
**Failure Probability**: 35%

**What could go wrong**:
- Contractors already use external tools for interconnection research
- "One more tool" fatigue (don't want features within Procore)
- RegGuard research is too generic (not specific enough for their project)
- Adoption rate is 3-5% (not 10-20% as projected)
- Solar installers stick to specialized tools (not Procore)

**Evidence this might fail**:
- Many contractors use specialized tools (SunPower, Solargis for solar)
- Integration features often have low adoption rates
- Contractors are creature of habit (prefer dedicated tools)
- Solar installers might have different needs than general contractors

**Impact if it fails**:
- Feature embedded but only 2-3% adoption instead of 10%+
- Revenue: 50K contractors × $3 × 2% adoption = $30K/month (not $90K)
- Deal still happens but much lower revenue ($300K vs $1M+)

### Critical Assumption #3: Revenue Model is Viable
**Failure Probability**: 45%

**What could go wrong**:
- They want to pay us a flat fee ($100K one-time) instead of revenue share
- They negotiate us down to 15% instead of 30-40%
- They bundle RegGuard free with base tier (no new revenue)
- Volume is lower (100K contractors, not 500K)
- Billing/payment issues (they don't pay us on time)

**Evidence this might fail**:
- Large companies often prefer fixed fees (easier accounting)
- They have negotiating power (we need them more than vice versa)
- They might see this as "cost of product" not "new revenue stream"
- Enterprise payment terms notoriously slow (90-120 day net)

**Impact if it fails**:
- Scenario 1: Flat fee ($100K one-time) = Much worse than $1M recurring
- Scenario 2: Lower rev-share (15% vs 30%) = $180K instead of $1M
- Scenario 3: Free bundle = $0 revenue from Procore

- Firecrawl might miss or misinterpret data

**Impact if it fails**:
- Contractors blame Procore when RegGuard data is wrong
- Legal liability (contractor sues, Procore sues us)
- Feature gets pulled
- Reputation damage in industry
- Partnership dissolves
