# PREMORTEM: The IC Consultant Pricing & Trial Strategy I Just Recommended

**Mode**: RIGOROUS PREMORTEM  
**Frame**: Challenge my own assumptions  
**Question**: What could go wrong with the pricing/trial model I just recommended?

---

## 🚨 THE BRUTAL TRUTH: MY STRATEGY MIGHT FAIL (40% probability)

I just told you:
- Charge IC consultants $3,500 per first project
- Offer 7-day limited trial
- Don't build FERC/RTO (it's "nice to have")
- Expect 50-60% conversion

**But what if I'm wrong?**

---

## 💡 CRITICAL FAILURE MODE #1: They Won't Pay $3,500 (50% probability)

**What I predicted:**
```
"$3,500 first project, they see ROI of $5-7K labor saved, they pay"
```

**What could actually happen:**
```
IC Consultant response:
"That's nice, but I'm not paying $3,500 until I see it working on a real project.
Let me use it free for one project, then I'll decide to pay going forward."

Translation: They want a free trial on a REAL project, not a demo.
```

**Why this breaks my model:**
- Free trial on real project = they own the results
- If results are mediocre, they never pay
- If results are great, they might copy it or negotiate down to $1,500
- You're taking the risk (they get value for free)
- Conversion drops from 50-60% to 20-30%

**Realistic scenario:**
```
Week 2: IC consultant says "OK let me try it free on a client project"
Week 3: They run RegGuard, get mediocre results (data quality issues, incomplete)
Week 4: They say "It's OK, but I think I can build this myself for $500"
Week 5: They never respond again

Your revenue: $0
Your loss: Time spent on demo + support
```

---

## 💡 CRITICAL FAILURE MODE #2: 7-Day Trial Is Too Short (40% probability)

**What I predicted:**
```
"7 days forces them to decide: is this worth $3,500? Yes/No"
```

**What could actually happen:**
```
IC Consultant: "Great tool! But I don't have a project right now.
Can I use it for 30 days? My next project starts in 3 weeks."

Translation: Your 7-day window expires before they can test.
```

**Why this breaks my model:**
- IC consultants work on project basis, not continuous
- Project flow is sporadic (might not have one this week)
- 7 days is too short for them to find a real project to test
- They'll ask for 30-day trial
- If you say no: They walk (and never come back)
- If you say yes: Now you're back to 30-day trial (which I said doesn't work)

**Realistic scenario:**
```
Week 1: IC consultant requests free trial
Week 2-3: They're busy with current projects, don't use it
Week 7: Trial expires without them testing
Week 8: They're interested again, but trial expired
Week 9: They forgot about you

Your conversion: 0%
```

---

## 💡 CRITICAL FAILURE MODE #3: FERC/RTO Is Actually THE Differentiator (30% probability)

**What I predicted:**
```
"FERC/RTO is 'nice to have', not needed for launch"
```

**What could actually happen:**
```
IC Consultant during demo: "This is good for site research, but what about 
regulatory changes? If FERC announces a new requirement mid-project, 
I need to know immediately."

Translation: FERC/RTO isn't a premium feature, it's table stakes.
```

**Why this breaks my model:**
- IC consultants LIVE in regulatory uncertainty
- They care deeply about unexpected rule changes
- Law firms probably offer regulatory monitoring (you don't)
- Without FERC/RTO, you look incomplete vs competitors
- It becomes THE reason they don't buy

**Realistic scenario:**
```
Demo conversation:
Them: "What about regulatory monitoring?"
You: "That's a premium feature we're adding later"
Them: "Oh, so I still have to monitor FERC myself?"
You: "Yes"
Them: "Then this is just research tool. I can use ChatGPT"

Your conversion: Drops from 50% to 20%
Their reasoning: RegGuard is incomplete without FERC/RTO
```

---

## 💡 CRITICAL FAILURE MODE #4: Market Is Smaller Than Expected (50% probability)

**What I predicted:**
```
"5-8 IC consultants by Month 4-6"
```

**What could actually happen:**
```
Real market:
├─ Specialist IC firms: ~20-30 in US (very small)
├─ Boutique regional IC consultants: ~50-100 (fragmented)
├─ Large engineering firms with IC practice: ~10-15
└─ Total reachable market: Maybe 80-150 people

Problem:
├─ 80-150 is VERY small market
├─ You need 5-8 to pay for this to work (6-10% of market)
├─ If you get 1 early, they might tell others "it didn't work for me"
└─ Word spreads quickly in small markets (negative OR positive)
```

**Why this breaks my model:**
- Small market = fewer chances to sell
- One bad customer = ruins reputation with remaining 74 people
- It's not like contractors (500K+ TAM), it's boutique consultants
- High failure rate (50%+ churn) means you need high volume
- You can't get volume in 150-person market

**Realistic scenario:**
```
Best case: You close 5 IC consultants
Worst case: 1 bad experience spreads to the other 70
Your reputation: Ruined in small market
Recovery: Impossible (they all know each other)

And with only 5 customers, you're not generating enough revenue to hire salespeople
or build FERC/RTO. You're stuck at $15-20K/year forever.
```

---

## 💡 CRITICAL FAILURE MODE #5: Pricing Negotiation Wars (60% probability)

**What I predicted:**
```
"$3,500 per project, annual subscription $2,500"
```

**What could actually happen:**
```
After closing 1-2 IC consultants:

IC Consultant #3: "I'll buy if you give me 50% off"
IC Consultant #4: "What if we do $2,000 per project?"
IC Consultant #5: "Can you do annual unlimited for $1,500?"

Translation: They talk to each other, compare notes, demand discounts.
```

**Why this breaks my model:**
- IC consultants are sophisticated buyers (they negotiate for living)
- They'll compare notes and demand parity
- Once you give one a discount, others find out and demand it too
- Your revenue collapses from $3.5K per to $1.5K per
- You can't sustain on $1.5K (doesn't cover support)

**Realistic scenario:**
```
Month 1: Close IC consultant for $3,500 ✓
Month 2: Close IC consultant for $3,000 (they negotiated)
Month 3: Close IC consultant for $2,000 (they negotiated hard)
Month 4: Close IC consultant for $1,500 (now it's a race to bottom)

Your revenue:
├─ Month 1: $3,500
├─ Month 2: $3,000
├─ Month 3: $2,000
├─ Month 4: $1,500
└─ Average: $2,500 (not $3,500!)

At $2,500 per project, you need 14+ projects to hit $35K revenue in Year 1.
Realistic: 5-8 projects = $12.5-20K revenue (not $25-35K I predicted)
```

## ✅ BOTTOM LINE

**My IC Consultant strategy is good, but my numbers are too optimistic.**

**What to actually do:**

1. **Price lower than I recommended** ($2,000-2,500, not $3,500)
   - Reason: They will negotiate, and law firm competitors are cheaper
   - Result: 40-50% conversion (not 50-60%)

2. **Extend trial to 30 days** (not 7)
   - Reason: Project cycles don't align with 7-day windows
   - Result: 30% more will get through trial period

3. **Launch FERC/RTO in Month 1, not Month 2+** (it's not optional)
   - Reason: Regulatory uncertainty is their #1 concern
   - Result: Conversion jumps from 40% to 50-60%

4. **Expect replication** (70% of IC consultants will build their own)
   - Reason: It's easy, they're smart, ROI is obvious
   - Defense: Network effects, reputation, integrations, ongoing updates

5. **Realistic Year 1 revenue: $12-20K** (not $25-35K)
   - 5-8 projects × $2,000-2,500 average
   - Some customers churn early (try, copy, leave)
   - Good news: If you hit $15K, you can hire someone part-time

6. **Only launch IC channel IF you have 4 weeks** (not 1 week)
   - Reason: You need FERC/RTO to be competitive
   - Effort: 2 weeks to build + 2 weeks to polish before outreach
